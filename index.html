About

I'm a second-year Master of Statistical Science student at Duke, graduating in May 2027. Before Duke I studied economics and statistics at Dongbei University of Finance and Economics.

My work sits between systematic equity research and machine learning for market microstructure. At Fubon Fund I built the factor-research pipeline and screening framework used to evaluate 101 alpha signals on the A-share market. At Duke I work with Dr. Alessio Brini on reinforcement learning for optimal trade execution, with a focus on reward design and on how learned policies transfer across liquidity and volatility regimes.

In both, most of my time goes into validation: transaction costs, held-out data, and markets that no longer look like the training set.

Fubon Fund

Quantitative research intern

Systematic equity-factor research on the A-share market: roughly 5,000 stocks, 12 years of data, 101 Alpha-style signals.

Pipeline
Built a vectorized Python factor-research pipeline covering all 101 signals. A full-universe backtest went from 6 hours to 20 minutes, which made same-day iteration on new signal ideas possible.
Screening
Designed a multi-stage screen (rank IC and ICIR, pairwise correlation filter at |ρ| < 0.6, Lasso and random-forest selection) that narrowed 101 raw signals to 14 low-redundancy candidates with mean rank IC of 0.035 and ICIR above 0.45.
After costs
Long-short portfolios showed a gross Sharpe of 1.6, falling to 0.4 net of 20 bps costs at 45% turnover. I flagged the short-horizon signals as capacity-constrained.
Attribution
Validated with Fama–MacBeth regressions and Fama–French attribution. Five factors kept residual alpha at t > 2.8 after size, value and momentum controls.
Outcome
Wrote six memos on signal stability, turnover sensitivity and out-of-sample decay. Two factors advanced to portfolio-manager evaluation.

Research

Reinforcement learning for optimal execution

Apr 2026–present

Graduate researcher, Duke University, advised by Dr. Alessio Brini

I train DQN execution agents in the ABIDES multi-agent limit-order-book simulator and study how the learned policies behave when liquidity and volatility shift away from what they were trained on.

Agent
The agent observes a five-dimensional rolling cost state: spread, book imbalance, price impact, realized slippage and top-5 depth. It is benchmarked against TWAP and Almgren–Chriss across 8 regimes and 4,800 paired held-out episodes.
Results
Execution cost is 34–37% below TWAP in normal conditions. On a held-out severe-illiquidity regime it is 72% below TWAP and 81% below Almgren–Chriss (paired t = −4.5).
Where it fails
Policies that dominate along the liquidity axis underperform TWAP by 30–50 bps on held-out high-volatility regimes. A separate transfer study over 30 policies (3 regimes × 5 seeds × 2 reward designs) shows the same asymmetry: volatility-trained policies pay about 2× the cost in calm markets, while the reverse transfer is close to free.
Reward gaming
Agents learned to leave 4–7% of the parent order unexecuted to exploit a linear completion penalty, which distorted cross-regime comparisons by up to 30%. I replaced the penalty with terminal settlement against the live book, using exact reward folding at γ = 1. Mean residual inventory fell from 22% to under 4%, and cross-seed dispersion under held-out volatility stress dropped 34–55%.

Beyond

Away from the terminal, I ski and surf whenever the season allows, and I spend much of my free time on photography. To me, photography is not simply about preserving a beautiful view or freezing a moment in time. Every photograph reflects a choice: what I noticed, what I left outside the frame, and why that particular scene made me pause. The moment I press the shutter often captures my own thoughts and emotions as much as it captures the subject in front of me. Sometimes a photo becomes meaningful only much later, when I look back and realize how closely it was tied to a certain period of my life. I enjoy the quiet attention that photography requires, especially the feeling of finding something worth remembering in an ordinary place. Through photographs, I can revisit not only what I saw, but also how I felt and who I was at that moment.
